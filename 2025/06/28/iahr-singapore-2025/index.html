FIELD NOTES / CONFERENCES

Reflections on the 41st IAHR World Congress in Singapore

2025.06.28TIANYU FU

From June 22 to 27, I attended the 41st IAHR World Congress at Singapore EXPO. The trip brought together several things I enjoy: sharing research, meeting people with different perspectives, and discovering a city through the moments between conference sessions. I came home with new ideas for my work, more confidence in presenting it, and a photograph that will always remind me of Singapore.

A day of discussion on nature-based solutions

On June 22, I joined Masterclass A: “Nature-based Solutions for Flooding and Water Management Resilience in a Changing Climate,” convened by Ellis Penning from Deltares and Catherine Wilson from Cardiff University. It was a welcoming start to the congress, with time to exchange ideas with the instructors and fellow researchers in a small group.

The course connected the design and placement of nature-based solutions with the research needed to evaluate them. Its programme paired morning discussions of hydraulic and hydrological processes in field and laboratory studies with an afternoon focus on modeling: the data required, the choice of spatial and temporal resolution, and the processes that different models can represent. Participants’ research projects formed the basis for discussion.

I enjoyed the opportunity to discuss research with people approaching water management from different backgrounds. The exchanges gave me a broader context for thinking about how hydrodynamics, ecological processes, and modeling can contribute to practical solutions. Getting to know the instructors and other participants, and taking a group photograph together afterwards, made the day especially memorable. I am grateful to Ellis and Catherine for creating this opportunity for discussion and connection.

Bringing my modeling work to Singapore

On June 23, I gave an oral presentation titled “Improving Water Quality Model Performance with Autocalibration: Assessing Impact of Hydrological Variability.”

The talk introduced EFDC-ACT, an automatic calibration toolkit for the EFDC model, and explored how the hydrological conditions represented in a calibration period can affect water quality predictions. Automatic calibration helps with the repeated work of updating parameters, running simulations, and evaluating results. Choosing suitable data for calibration remains an equally important part of the process.

Using Yuqiao Reservoir as a case study, I compared an existing model calibrated and validated using two dry years with recalibration over 2006–2015, a period spanning dry, normal, and wet conditions. In this case, the broader calibration period improved water quality performance. The experience reinforced a point I wanted to communicate: when assessing a model, we need to look closely at the range of conditions it has been asked to reproduce.

It was rewarding to explain this work to researchers beyond my usual circle. Preparing the talk and answering questions in English also helped me become more comfortable discussing both the methods and the reasoning behind them. For more technical background, I have written a separate post about EFDC-ACT and automatic model calibration.

A little of Singapore to take home

Singapore struck me as a clean and elegant city. Away from the conference, one of my favourite moments was visiting Sentosa and taking a photograph at the “Southernmost Point of Continental Asia” landmark. With the bay and palm trees behind me and my camera around my neck, it became one of my favourite photographs of myself—a small moment from the trip that I am very glad to have kept.

Before leaving, I also stopped at the airport’s Pokémon Center to buy gifts for friends. Bringing home something chosen for them was a lovely way to end the trip and share a little of the experience.

I am grateful to the organizers, speakers, and everyone who took the time to talk with me. What I will remember most is the combination of useful scientific conversations and simple personal moments: presenting my work, getting to know fellow researchers, that photograph on Sentosa, and choosing gifts on the way home. Together, they made this a very happy week in Singapore.
