Shared Ideas, New Friends: Two Days in Shenzhen

2026.05.16TIANYU FU

On May 15–16, I joined colleagues from Tianjin University’s ecohydraulics team at Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen, for the 10th Academic Forum on Water Environment Modeling and Prediction. These two days gave me plenty to be happy about: the opportunity to share my research, an award to celebrate with our team, and new friends who share an interest in understanding water environments.

Sharing the work behind the slides

My presentation focused on developing and applying automatic calibration and evaluation techniques for three-dimensional hydrodynamic and water quality models. It was a chance to bring work from my daily research into a room full of people interested in similar questions.

Much of this work happens at a desk, through code, model runs, and repeated checks of results. Presenting it to others is a different kind of reward. I was delighted to share what we have been working on and hear perspectives from beyond our own group. Explaining the work also encouraged me to think about how to communicate its purpose more clearly.

For readers interested in the technical background, I have written separately about EFDC-ACT and our work on automatic model calibration.

A celebration for the team

I was very happy to receive second prize in the doctoral category of the postgraduate forum. I am grateful for this encouragement, and for the guidance of my advisor, Professor Chen Zhang, and the support of our team throughout the research.

There was more good news to share: both of our master’s student participants received awards, earning a second prize and an excellence award. Celebrating together made the occasion especially memorable. Professors Zhouhu Wu, Daobin Ji, and Chen Zhang also received awards recognizing their outstanding contributions over the forum’s first decade.

New friends, and more to think about

One of the happiest parts of the trip was meeting new friends. It felt good to connect with fellow researchers, learn about their work, and find interests we have in common. I hope we will keep in touch and continue these conversations as our research develops.

The talks also gave me plenty to think about. Hearing different approaches to water environment modeling and prediction was a reminder of how much there is to learn across this field. I came away with renewed enthusiasm for my own work and curiosity about the questions others are pursuing.

Thank you, Shenzhen

My sincere thanks go to Professor Yiping Li, who initiated the forum and has sustained it through ten editions, creating opportunities for academic exchange and young researchers. I am also grateful to Professor Jiping Jiang, the volunteers at HIT Shenzhen, and the organizing committee for making everyone feel welcome and keeping the meeting running smoothly.

With the flame trees in bloom, Shenzhen provided a lovely setting for these two days. I will remember the pleasure of sharing our work, celebrating together, and getting to know new people. There is more research to do and much to look forward to. See you in Zhengzhou!
